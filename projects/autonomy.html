HARDWARE · SOFTWARE INTEGRATION · JOHNS HOPKINS APL

Drone Autonomy System Development

Mechanical design and computer-vision-based landing integration work supporting autonomous UAV operations.

← All projects

Overview

Developed during my work at the Johns Hopkins University Applied Physics Laboratory (JHU APL), this project sought to integrate a ground vehicle with a fleet of FPV (First-Person View) drones, creating a multi-agent autonomous platform.

Note on Documentation: Due to security guidelines associated with my work at JHU APL, images, raw datasets, code, and CAD schematics cannot be publicly displayed.

My Contribution

Mechanical & Hardware Design

  • Rotating Landing Mechanism: Designed a custom multi-drone landing platform in SolidWorks to house six FPV drones on top of an active UGV platform.
  • CAD Iteration & DFM: Iterated through 8 design revisions to accommodate FPV drone landing tolerances, optimize mechanical assembly, and ensure component 3D printability.
  • Hardware Selection: Passed technical design review and specified electronic components, motor actuators, and mechanical fasteners for system fabrication.

Computer Vision & Software Integration

  • Drone Hardware Integration: Deconstructed drone hardware to connect flight controller and visual transmitter to receiver and ground station to bypass compute limitations onboard the FPV.
  • Autonomous Landing Pipeline: Developed computer vision algorithm using OpenCV and ArUco tags, enabling FPV drones to execute autonomous precision landing sequences.
  • Ground Station Communication: Programmed software linking ground station processing nodes directly with drone flight controllers for real-time vector guidance.

Key Takeaways

Gained hands-on experience in mechatronics system design, working with various drone components, and developing computer vision pipelines for real-time robotic platforms.
